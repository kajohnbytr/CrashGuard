CRASHGUARD - ESP32-C3 SUPER MINI WIRING
=======================================

Target: ESP32-C3 Super Mini (common board exposing GPIO0-GPIO10)
Firmware environment: esp32-c3-super-mini
Firmware build: platformio run -e esp32-c3-super-mini

This pin plan uses only seven GPIOs. GPS is receive-only, leaving GPIO4 spare.
Because seller revisions vary, verify the labels and schematic of your exact
board before connecting power.

COMPACT ASSEMBLY
----------------

Do not use a solderless breadboard in the helmet. Use either:

- a small custom carrier PCB (preferred), or
- a soldered wire harness with insulated junctions and locking connectors.

The 3V3 and GND pins are shared rails, not one-device-only connections. Split
them at a tiny soldered distribution point or on the carrier PCB. Do not try
to force several loose wires into one ESP32 header hole.

Recommended compact power topology:

  protected battery / documented power-path circuit
                 |
                 +--> regulated system rail --> ESP32-C3 5V/VIN*
                 |                         +--> GPS VCC*
                 |
                 +--> dedicated 3.4-4.4 V, >=2 A modem regulator
                                           +--> SIM800L VCC

  ESP32-C3 3V3 ----------------------------+--> MPU6050 VCC

  common ground junction ------------------+--> ESP32-C3 GND
                                           +--> MPU6050 GND
                                           +--> GPS GND
                                           +--> buzzer-driver GND
                                           +--> modem regulator GND
                                           +--> SIM800L GND

* Verify the exact board and GPS breakout. A pin marked 5V/VBUS is not always
  a wide-range VIN input. Never connect a raw Li-ion cell to 3V3.

Keep the battery-to-modem-regulator and regulator-to-SIM800L wires short and
thicker than the signal wiring. Place the modem bulk capacitor directly beside
the SIM800L. Route the MPU6050 wiring away from the modem power and antenna.

For serviceability, use small locking connectors such as JST-PH only where
their current rating is adequate. The SIM800L supply path may require a higher-
current connector than the sensor connections. Add strain relief and fully
insulate every soldered junction with heat-shrink tubing.


SIGNAL WIRING
-------------

ESP32-C3 SUPER MINI      PERIPHERAL
-------------------      ----------------------------------------------
GPIO5                    MPU6050 SDA
GPIO6                    MPU6050 SCL
3V3                      MPU6050 VCC (only for a 3.3 V-safe breakout)
GND                      MPU6050 GND

GPIO3 (UART0 RX)         GPS TX
Not connected            GPS RX (not required for normal NMEA reception)
Suitable supply          GPS VCC (verify the exact breakout)
GND                      GPS GND

GPIO0 (UART1 RX)         SIM800L TXD
GPIO1 (UART1 TX)         SIM800L RXD (level adaptation may be required)
GND                      SIM800L GND

GPIO7                    Buzzer transistor/MOSFET driver input
GND                      Buzzer driver ground

GPIO10                   One side of cancel button
GND                      Other side of cancel button

USB connector            Programming and native USB Serial Monitor
GPIO4                    Spare


PIN USE SUMMARY
---------------

Used:       GPIO0, GPIO1, GPIO3, GPIO5, GPIO6, GPIO7, GPIO10
Spare:      GPIO4
Avoid:      GPIO2, GPIO8, GPIO9 (boot-strapping pins)
Reserved:   GPIO18, GPIO19 (native USB D-/D+; usually not broken out)
Do not use: GPIO12-GPIO17 (normally connected to flash on ESP32-C3)

Do not attach external circuits to GPIO2, GPIO8, or GPIO9 unless their reset
levels are fully controlled. GPIO9 is commonly connected to the BOOT button.


POWER AND LEVEL REQUIREMENTS
----------------------------

1. ESP32-C3 GPIO is 3.3 V only and is not 5 V tolerant.

2. Power the SIM800L from its own stable supply. A bare module normally needs
   about 3.4-4.4 V and current bursts near 2 A. Do not power it from the C3's
   3V3 pin or from an unverified 5 V source. Put suitable low-ESR bulk
   capacitance close to the modem (commonly 1000-2200 uF plus 100 nF).

3. Join ESP32, MPU6050, GPS, SIM800L supply, and buzzer-driver grounds. Keep
   the SIM800L power and return wiring short and heavy.

4. Check the SIM800L carrier's UART levels. Its TXD is commonly safe for a
   3.3 V input, but the ESP32 GPIO1-to-SIM800L RXD path may require a divider
   or proper level shifter. Follow the exact module documentation.

5. Use a transistor or logic-level MOSFET driver for the buzzer. Do not power
   a buzzer directly from GPIO7. Add a flyback diode for an inductive load.

6. Confirm that GPS TX and the MPU6050 I2C pull-ups never exceed 3.3 V.


FIRST POWER-UP
--------------

[ ] Disconnect power while wiring.
[ ] Confirm the exact board pin labels and USB connector orientation.
[ ] Meter all supply rails and polarity before connecting modules.
[ ] Initially leave the SIM800L supply disconnected.
[ ] Build/upload the esp32-c3-super-mini PlatformIO environment.
[ ] Use 115200 baud and enable USB CDC on boot.
[ ] Verify MPU6050 and GPS operation first.
[ ] Connect the separately powered SIM800L only after checking common ground,
    UART levels, antenna, SIM, and modem supply stability.

The first-boot Wi-Fi network is "SmartHelmet-Setup".
Open http://192.168.4.1/ while connected to that network.

This is a bench prototype, not certified safety equipment.
